Module Topics

Introduction to AI-Generated Presentations

Overview of how the Module Editor uses AI to automatically structure PowerPoint slide decks from module topics. Learners are introduced to the presentation generation feature and its purpose within the course creation workflow.
  • What Is an AI-Generated Presentation? — An AI-generated presentation is a PowerPoint slide deck automatically structured by artificial intelligence based on the topics within a module.
  • Where the Feature Lives: The Module Editor — The presentation generation feature is accessed directly within the Module Editor, the central workspace for building and managing course modules.
  • How Module Topics Drive the Slide Deck — The AI uses the module's individual topics as the foundational input for determining the structure and content of the generated slide deck.
  • Purpose Within the Course Creation Workflow — AI-generated presentations are designed to accelerate the course development process by automating a time-intensive step in instructional design.
  • Overview of the Two Presentation Modes — The Module Editor offers two presentation generation options: a standard AI-structured slide deck and a narrated presentation with embedded audio.

Generating a PowerPoint Slide Deck from Module Topics

Step-by-step walkthrough of initiating and completing the AI-driven slide deck generation process within the Module Editor. Covers how module topics are mapped and transformed into organized PowerPoint slides.
  • Accessing the Slide Deck Generator in the Module Editor — The slide deck generation process begins inside the Module Editor, where the AI-driven tool is available once your module topics are defined.
  • How Module Topics Are Mapped to Slides — Each module topic is analyzed by the AI and transformed into one or more corresponding PowerPoint slides, preserving the logical structure of the module.
  • Initiating the AI-Driven Generation Process — Once the module topics are ready, the learner triggers the AI generation process, which automatically structures and populates the slide deck.
  • Reviewing the Organized Slide Structure — After generation completes, the slide deck is presented in an organized format that reflects the module's instructional design.
  • Completing and Exporting the PowerPoint Slide Deck — The final step in the process involves completing the generation and exporting the finished slide deck as a PowerPoint file.

Understanding the Narrated Presentation Option

Introduction to the narrated presentation feature and how it differs from a standard generated presentation. Explains the end-to-end concept of combining slides, speaker notes, audio, and timing into a single cohesive output.
  • What Is a Narrated Presentation? — A narrated presentation is an enhanced version of a standard generated slide deck that combines visual slides with spoken audio and precise timing.
  • The Role of Speaker Notes in Narration — Speaker notes serve as the script for the narrated presentation, providing the text that will be converted into spoken audio for each slide.
  • Text-to-Speech Conversion — The narrated presentation feature uses text-to-speech technology to automatically convert written speaker notes into spoken audio.
  • Slide Timing and Synchronization — Timing is embedded into the narrated presentation so that each slide advances automatically in sync with its corresponding audio narration.
  • A Single Cohesive Output — The narrated presentation bundles slides, speaker notes, audio tracks, and timing data into one unified output file.
  • How the Narrated Option Differs from a Standard Presentation — While both options generate AI-structured slide decks from module topics, the narrated option adds the audio and timing layer that a standard presentation lacks.

Writing Per-Slide Speaker Notes

Explains how speaker notes are authored or AI-generated for each individual slide within the narrated presentation workflow. Covers best practices for effective, clear narration text that aligns with slide content.
  • Purpose of Speaker Notes in Narrated Presentations — Speaker notes serve as the script for the audio narration that accompanies each slide, giving learners a spoken explanation of the on-screen content.
  • AI-Generated vs. Manually Authored Notes — Speaker notes can either be automatically generated by the AI based on the module's topic content or written and edited manually by the course author.
  • Writing Notes in a Conversational, Spoken Style — Because notes are converted directly to speech, they should be written the way a person naturally speaks rather than in a formal written style.
  • Aligning Notes with Slide Visual Content — Effective speaker notes expand on or contextualize the information shown visually on the slide rather than reading out every word already visible to the learner.
  • Controlling Length and Pacing Through Notes — The length of each slide's speaker notes determines how long that slide remains on screen, making note length a key tool for controlling presentation pacing.
  • Reviewing and Refining Notes Before Audio Conversion — Before triggering the text-to-speech conversion, authors should review all per-slide notes to check for clarity, accuracy, and consistency across the full presentation.

Converting Speaker Notes to Spoken Audio with Text-to-Speech

Covers the process of transforming written speaker notes into spoken audio using Google Cloud Text-to-Speech. Learners explore available voice options and how the conversion is applied across slides.
  • What Is Text-to-Speech Conversion in This Context — Text-to-speech (TTS) conversion transforms the written speaker notes associated with each slide into synthesized spoken audio using Google Cloud Text-to-Speech.
  • Writing Speaker Notes for TTS Compatibility — To achieve natural-sounding audio output, speaker notes should be written in clear, complete sentences that are optimized for speech rather than visual reading.
  • Selecting a Voice from Available TTS Options — The platform offers a selection of TTS voice options, allowing learners to choose the voice that best fits the tone and audience of their presentation.
  • Applying TTS Conversion Across All Slides — Once a voice is selected, the TTS conversion is applied across all slides in the presentation, processing each slide's speaker notes into its own audio segment.
  • How TTS Audio Is Embedded into Slides — After conversion, the generated audio is embedded directly into each slide, synchronizing the narration with the slide's display timing.
  • Reviewing and Regenerating TTS Audio — After initial conversion, learners can review the generated audio for each slide and regenerate narration for any slides where the speaker notes have been updated.

Embedding Audio and Timing into Slides

Details how the generated audio is synchronized with each slide and embedded into the final PowerPoint file. Explains how timing is set so narration plays in alignment with the corresponding slide content.
  • How Generated Audio is Linked to Individual Slides — Each slide in the narrated presentation receives its own dedicated audio clip, generated from that slide's speaker notes using text-to-speech.
  • Text-to-Speech Conversion Process — The system converts the written speaker notes for each slide into spoken audio using a text-to-speech engine before embedding begins.
  • Embedding Audio Directly into the PowerPoint File — Once generated, the audio clips are embedded inside the PowerPoint file itself, making the narration portable and self-contained.
  • Setting Slide Timing to Match Narration Length — Slide timing is configured so that each slide remains visible for exactly the duration of its corresponding narration audio clip.
  • Synchronization Between Narration and Slide Content — Synchronization ensures that what the viewer hears aligns precisely with the slide content being displayed at that moment.
  • Result: A Self-Playing Narrated Presentation — The combination of embedded audio and automatic timing produces a finished presentation that plays through on its own without requiring a live presenter.
