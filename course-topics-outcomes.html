Module 1: Introduction to PreseMaker: What It Is and How It Works

Topics

What Is PreseMaker?

Introduces PreseMaker as an AI-assisted course authoring tool for Windows, outlining its core purpose and the key problems it is designed to solve.
  • Definition of PreseMaker — PreseMaker is an AI-assisted course authoring tool designed to run on Windows operating systems.
  • Who PreseMaker Is Designed For — PreseMaker targets educators, trainers, and instructional designers who need to create structured learning materials efficiently.
  • Core Purpose of PreseMaker — The primary purpose of PreseMaker is to take users from a blank starting point to fully populated course materials in a structured, guided way.
  • Key Problems PreseMaker Solves — PreseMaker addresses common pain points in course authoring, including inconsistent structure, slow content generation, and the difficulty of organizing large amounts of material.
  • AI Assistance as a Defining Feature — The AI component of PreseMaker is central to what sets it apart from conventional authoring tools.
  • Windows Platform Context — PreseMaker is specifically built for the Windows environment, meaning its features and interface are optimized for that platform.

Who Is PreseMaker For?

Identifies the target users of PreseMaker, including instructional designers, trainers, and educators, and explains how the tool addresses their specific authoring challenges.
  • Instructional Designers — Instructional designers are a primary audience for PreseMaker, as the tool is built to streamline the complex process of structuring and authoring course content.
  • Corporate Trainers — Corporate trainers who need to develop and deliver training programs quickly will find PreseMaker addresses their time and resource constraints.
  • Educators and Academic Professionals — Educators at schools, colleges, and universities can use PreseMaker to author structured course content without requiring deep technical expertise.
  • Common Authoring Challenges PreseMaker Addresses — Across all user types, PreseMaker is designed to solve recurring pain points that instructional designers, trainers, and educators face when creating course content.
  • Why a Windows-Based Tool Matters for These Users — PreseMaker is specifically built for Windows, which reflects the environment in which many instructional designers, trainers, and educators already work.

The Course → Module → Content Hierarchy

Explains the three-level organizational structure that PreseMaker uses to arrange learning materials, clarifying how courses, modules, and content items relate to one another.
  • The Three-Level Structure Overview — PreseMaker organizes all learning materials into a strict three-level hierarchy: Course, Module, and Content. This structure provides a consistent framework for arranging instructional materials from the broadest category down to individual pieces of content.
  • The Course Level — A Course is the highest organizational unit in PreseMaker and serves as the overall container for an entire subject or training program. It defines the broad scope of what learners will study.
  • The Module Level — Modules are the mid-tier organizational units that break a Course into manageable, thematically related sections. Each Module groups Content items that share a common topic or learning goal.
  • The Content Level — Content items are the individual learning assets that live inside Modules and represent the actual instructional material learners interact with. This is where the core teaching and assessment activities reside.
  • How the Levels Relate to One Another — The three levels are interdependent — a Content item cannot exist without a Module, and a Module cannot exist without a Course. This parent-child relationship ensures that all materials remain organized and contextually connected.
  • Why This Hierarchy Matters for Course Authoring — The Course → Module → Content hierarchy is not just an organizational choice — it is the foundation of how PreseMaker manages, generates, and displays learning materials. Understanding it is essential before beginning any authoring work.

  • Defining the Course Structure — After the blank course exists, the next step is establishing its architecture by adding modules — the mid-level groupings that organize related content together.
  • Understanding the Course → Module → Content Hierarchy — PreseMaker organizes all learning materials within a strict three-level hierarchy: Course at the top, Modules in the middle, and Content items at the bottom.
  • Generating Content with AI Assistance — Once the structure is in place, PreseMaker's AI engine can generate fully developed learning materials to populate each module.
  • Reviewing and Populating Generated Materials — After AI generation, the workflow shifts to a human review phase where authors examine, refine, and finalize the content that has been produced.
  • Completing the Workflow: From Blank to Fully Built — The PreseMaker workflow concludes when all modules contain reviewed, polished content — transforming the original blank course into a complete, ready-to-use training program.

How AI Assistance Works in PreseMaker

Describes the role of AI within PreseMaker, highlighting how it accelerates and supports content creation throughout the authoring process.
  • AI as a Content Acceleration Engine — PreseMaker uses AI assistance to significantly speed up the course authoring process, reducing the time authors spend generating content from scratch.
  • AI Support Throughout the Authoring Workflow — AI assistance is embedded across the entire authoring workflow in PreseMaker, not limited to a single step or phase.
  • Problem-Solving Role of AI in PreseMaker — PreseMaker's AI assistance directly addresses common pain points experienced by course authors, particularly around content creation efficiency.
  • AI as a Supportive, Not Autonomous, Tool — The AI in PreseMaker is designed to support and augment human authoring rather than operate independently or make final decisions.

Module 3: AI Providers and API Keys: Setting Up Your Connection

Topics

How PreseMaker Connects to AI Providers

Explains the relationship between PreseMaker and external AI services, and why an external connection is necessary to power AI-driven features.
  • PreseMaker as an AI-Powered Application — PreseMaker is an AI-assisted course authoring application that uses artificial intelligence to generate course content — presentations, text lessons, quizzes, assignments, and interactive activities.
  • What External AI Providers Are — External AI providers are third-party companies that host and operate large language models accessible via the internet.
  • Why a Direct Connection to AI Providers Is Necessary — PreseMaker must establish a direct connection to an AI provider in order to send your content requests and receive AI-generated responses in real time.
  • The Role of API Keys in the Connection — An API key is a unique credential that authenticates PreseMaker's requests to an external AI provider on your behalf.
  • Choosing Your AI Provider — Because PreseMaker supports multiple AI providers, users have the flexibility to connect to the service that best suits their needs and preferences.

Overview of Supported AI Providers

Introduces the four supported AI providers — Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, OpenAI, and Mistral — and highlights key differences learners should know when choosing one.
  • What Is an AI Provider? — An AI provider is an external company that hosts and operates large language models accessible through the internet via an API connection.
  • Anthropic Claude — Anthropic Claude is an AI provider known for producing detailed, nuanced, and safety-focused responses.
  • Google Gemini — Google Gemini is Google's flagship AI model family, integrated with Google's broader ecosystem and research capabilities.
  • OpenAI — OpenAI is one of the most widely recognized AI providers, offering the GPT series of models used by millions of developers and applications worldwide.
  • Mistral — Mistral is a European AI provider offering efficient, open-weight models that are competitive in performance while often being more cost-effective.
  • Key Differences to Consider When Choosing a Provider — While all four providers can power PreseMaker's features, learners should evaluate them across a few important dimensions before selecting one.

Understanding API Keys

Defines what an API key is, why it is required to authenticate requests to an AI provider, and how it acts as the credential linking PreseMaker to the chosen service.
  • What Is an API Key? — An API key is a unique alphanumeric string issued by an AI provider that identifies and authorizes your application to use their service.
  • Why API Keys Are Required — AI providers require API keys to control and authenticate access to their models, ensuring only authorized users can send requests.
  • The API Key as a Credential — The API key acts as the primary credential that links PreseMaker to your chosen AI provider account.
  • API Keys Are Provider-Specific — Each supported AI provider — Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, OpenAI, and Mistral — issues its own separate API key.
  • How the Key Connects PreseMaker to the Service — When PreseMaker makes an AI request, it attaches your API key to that request so the provider can authenticate and process it.

Obtaining Your API Key

Walks learners through the process of creating an account with an AI provider and generating an API key ready for use in PreseMaker.
  • Choosing an AI Provider — Before generating an API key, you must decide which AI provider you want to connect to PreseMaker.
  • Creating an Account with Your Chosen Provider — To obtain an API key, you first need to register for an account on your chosen provider's official website.
  • Navigating to the API Key Section — Once logged in to your provider account, you need to locate the area where API keys are created and managed.
  • Generating a New API Key — With the API Keys section open, you can create a new key that PreseMaker will use to authenticate requests to the AI provider.
  • Copying and Securing Your API Key — Immediately after generation, you must copy your API key and store it securely, as most providers will not display it again.
  • Confirming Your Key Is Ready for Use in PreseMaker — Before entering the key in PreseMaker, take a moment to verify that the key has been successfully created and is in an active state.

Entering Your API Key in PreseMaker Settings

Guides learners step by step through locating the Settings screen in PreseMaker and correctly entering their API key to activate the AI connection.
  • Navigating to the PreseMaker Settings Screen — Before entering your API key, you need to locate the Settings screen within PreseMaker where AI provider configuration options are found.
  • Selecting Your AI Provider — PreseMaker supports multiple AI providers, and the AI Provider Keys settings show a field for each one together — you do not select or activate a provider first; you simply paste each key into its own field.
  • Entering the API Key Correctly — Accurate entry of your API key is critical — even a single incorrect character will prevent PreseMaker from establishing a successful AI connection.
  • Saving and Confirming the API Key Entry — After entering your API key, you must save the settings so PreseMaker can store and activate the connection to your chosen AI provider.
  • Understanding Local Encryption of Your API Key — PreseMaker encrypts and stores your API key locally on your device, ensuring that your credentials are protected and not transmitted to external servers beyond the intended AI provider.
  • Troubleshooting Common Key Entry Issues — If PreseMaker does not recognize your API key after entry, a few common issues are typically responsible and can be resolved quickly.

API Key Encryption and Local Storage

Explains how PreseMaker protects user API keys through encryption and stores them locally, addressing common privacy and security concerns.
  • What Is Local Storage for API Keys? — PreseMaker stores your API keys directly on your own device rather than transmitting them to any remote server or cloud database.
  • How Encryption Protects Your API Keys — Before saving an API key to local storage, PreseMaker encrypts it so that the raw key value is never written in a readable format.
  • Why PreseMaker Does Not Store Keys on a Server — Keeping API keys off external servers eliminates an entire category of risk — large-scale data breaches that expose many users' credentials at once.
  • Addressing Common Privacy Concerns — Users often wonder whether entering an API key into a third-party application is safe; PreseMaker's architecture is specifically designed to answer those concerns.
  • User Responsibilities for Key Security — While PreseMaker encrypts and stores keys securely, users still play an important role in maintaining the overall security of their API credentials.

Module 4: Configuring AI Providers per Task and Using the Prompt Button

Topics

Introduction to AI Providers in PreseMaker

An overview of the four AI providers supported by PreseMaker and how they are used to handle text-based generation tasks. This topic establishes the foundational context for configuring and working with AI providers.
  • What PreseMaker Uses AI Providers For — PreseMaker relies on AI providers exclusively for text-based generation tasks across the course-building workflow.
  • The Four Supported AI Providers — PreseMaker supports exactly four AI providers: Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google), GPT-4o (OpenAI), and Mistral AI.
  • How API Keys Are Stored and Secured — When you enter an API key into PreseMaker's Settings, the application encrypts and stores it securely on your local machine.
  • The Active AI Provider Setting — PreseMaker uses a single Active AI Provider setting in Settings to determine which provider handles all AI requests.
  • One Provider Handles Every Task — No Per-Task Routing — There is no mechanism in PreseMaker to assign different AI providers to different types of tasks — one active provider serves all tasks universally.

Obtaining and Entering API Keys

How to acquire an API key from a supported AI provider and enter it in PreseMaker's Settings. This topic also covers how keys are securely stored using Windows DPAPI and what minimum key requirements exist.
  • The Four Supported AI Providers — PreseMaker is compatible with four AI providers, each requiring its own API key obtained directly from the provider's platform.
  • Acquiring an API Key from a Provider — To obtain an API key, you must create a developer or API account with your chosen AI provider and generate a key through their platform.
  • Entering Your API Key in PreseMaker Settings — Once you have your API key, you enter it in PreseMaker's Settings panel, where it is associated with the corresponding provider.
  • Secure Storage with Windows DPAPI — PreseMaker encrypts every API key using Windows Data Protection API (DPAPI) before storing it, ensuring keys are protected at rest on your machine.
  • Keys Are Never Shared or Stored in Course Files — API keys remain entirely local to your machine and are never embedded in course content or transmitted to a repository.
  • Minimum Key Requirements — You do not need to configure API keys for all four providers — a single valid key for one provider is the minimum requirement to enable AI features in PreseMaker.

The Active AI Provider Setting

An explanation of the single Active AI Provider dropdown in Settings and how the selected provider handles every AI task in PreseMaker. This topic clarifies that one provider is responsible for all generation work simultaneously.
  • What the Active AI Provider Setting Is — In PreseMaker's Settings, there is a single dropdown called the Active AI Provider, which determines which AI service handles all generation work in the application.
  • One Provider Covers Every AI Task — The selected Active AI Provider handles all text-based generation tasks in PreseMaker simultaneously — there is no division of responsibility between providers.
  • No Per-Task Provider Assignment Exists — PreseMaker does not allow you to assign different AI providers to different types of tasks; the application has no routing table or per-task dropdown of any kind.
  • Why a Single Active Provider Simplifies Configuration — The single Active AI Provider model was designed to eliminate the confusion that arose when different tasks were silently routed to different providers, which could cause unexpected failures.
  • Choosing Which Provider to Make Active — PreseMaker supports four AI providers — Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google), GPT-4o (OpenAI), and Mistral AI — and you select the one you want to use from the Active AI Provider dropdown after entering its API key.

Understanding That There Is No Per-Task Provider Assignment

A focused clarification that PreseMaker does not support routing different task types to different providers, and why that feature was removed. This topic corrects common misconceptions and explains the simplified single-provider model.
  • The Single-Provider Rule — PreseMaker uses exactly one active AI provider for every task it performs, with no exceptions.
  • What Per-Task Assignment Means — and Why It Does Not Exist — Per-task provider assignment would mean routing specific task types — such as quizzes or narration — to different AI providers, but PreseMaker does not support this.
  • Why the Feature Was Removed — An earlier version of PreseMaker did include per-task provider assignment, but it was deliberately removed because it caused significant user confusion.
  • Correcting Common Misconceptions — Because per-task routing existed in an older version, some users arrive with the expectation that different tasks can still be sent to different providers.
  • Practical Implication: One Key Is All You Need — Because every task uses the same provider, you only need to obtain and configure a single API key to use all of PreseMaker's AI features.

Using the Prompt Button to Inspect AI Requests

How to use the Prompt button to view the exact text sent to the active AI provider before or during generation. This topic helps learners understand and verify the input their chosen provider receives.
  • What the Prompt Button Does — The Prompt button allows you to inspect the exact text that PreseMaker will send to your active AI provider before or during generation.
  • When to Use the Prompt Button — The Prompt button is most useful when you want to verify, troubleshoot, or simply understand the input being sent before committing to a full generation.
  • Understanding the Text Shown in the Prompt — All AI tasks in PreseMaker are text-based, so every prompt you inspect will be a text request — there are no image or vision requests.
  • The Prompt Reflects Your Active Provider Selection — Because PreseMaker uses a single active AI provider for all tasks, the prompt you inspect is always destined for that one selected provider.
  • Verifying Input to Diagnose Generation Issues — The Prompt button is a practical diagnostic tool when AI output does not match your expectations, letting you rule out input-side problems.

Interpreting and Responding to Generation Failures

What generation failure messages mean and how to respond to them effectively. This topic guides learners through diagnosing common causes of failed AI requests and taking corrective action.
  • What a Generation Failure Means — A generation failure occurs when PreseMaker sends a request to the active AI provider and does not receive a successful response.
  • Common Cause: Missing or Invalid API Key — The most frequent cause of a generation failure is that the API key for the active provider is absent, incorrectly entered, or has been revoked by the provider.
  • Common Cause: Wrong Active Provider Selected — PreseMaker uses a single Active AI Provider for every task, so selecting a provider whose key has not been entered will cause all AI requests to fail.
  • Common Cause: Provider-Side Issues — Even with a correct key and active provider, failures can occur due to outages, rate limits, or quota exhaustion on the provider's platform.
  • Diagnosing the Failure: A Step-by-Step Approach — Effective diagnosis follows a consistent order: verify the active provider, verify the key, then look outward to the provider's platform.
  • Taking Corrective Action — Once the cause is identified, corrective actions are straightforward and typically require only a Settings update or a provider account action.

  • The Remember Level: Recalling Foundational Knowledge — The Remember level is the foundation of Bloom's Taxonomy, focusing on a learner's ability to retrieve previously learned information from memory.
  • The Understand Level: Making Sense of Information — The Understand level asks learners to interpret, classify, or explain ideas and concepts in their own words, demonstrating genuine comprehension.

Understanding the Tiered Status System

Describes the four possible status tiers a standard can hold — not aligned, not addressed, practiced only, and assessed — and what conditions determine each tier.
  • The Four Status Tiers Defined — Every mapped standard is assigned one of four discrete status tiers that describe how thoroughly it is covered in the course.
  • Not Aligned Status — A standard receives the 'not aligned' status when absolutely nothing in the course has been mapped to it.
  • Not Addressed Status — A standard is 'not addressed' when it has been mapped to the course but no specific assignment, quiz item, or activity is aligned to it.
  • Practiced Only Status — A standard reaches 'practiced only' status when the sole aligned materials are activities — with no aligned assignments or quiz items present.
  • Assessed Status — A standard achieves 'assessed' status as soon as at least one aligned assignment or aligned quiz item exists for it.
  • How Conditions Determine Tier Transitions — The status tier a standard holds is directly determined by the type and presence of materials aligned to it, following a strict hierarchy.

Assessment vs. Practice: Key Distinctions

Clarifies the precise difference between what counts as assessment (assignments and quiz items) and what counts as practice (activities only), and how this affects a standard's status.
  • The Two Categories: Assessment and Practice — The Standards Coverage view divides aligned course materials into exactly two categories: assessment and practice. Understanding which category each material type belongs to is essential for interpreting a standard's status correctly.
  • What Makes a Standard 'Assessed' — A standard reaches the 'assessed' status as soon as at least one aligned assignment or aligned quiz item exists in the course. No additional steps are required to move it into this status.
  • What Makes a Standard 'Practiced Only' — A standard shows a status of 'practiced only' when the sole aligned material is an activity — meaning no assignment or quiz item has been aligned to it yet.
  • The Lower Tiers: Not Aligned and Not Addressed — Before a standard can be practiced or assessed, it must first be mapped and then have material aligned to it. Two lower-tier statuses reflect gaps at these earlier stages.
  • How Status Affects Coverage Reporting — The headline summary in the Standards Coverage view reflects how many standards are actually assessed, making the assessment-versus-practice distinction directly relevant to the reported coverage percentage.
  • Common Misstatements to Avoid — Two specific errors frequently arise when describing assessment versus practice distinctions, and both must be explicitly avoided when communicating about standards coverage.

How the Cognitive-Depth Check Works

Explains that PreseMaker compares a standard's Bloom's level against the highest Bloom's level of aligned learning outcomes, not materials, to determine whether a cognitive-depth gap exists.
  • What a Cognitive-Depth Gap Is — A cognitive-depth gap exists when a standard demands a higher Bloom's level than any of its aligned learning outcomes reach.
  • The Comparison Is Between Standard and Learning Outcomes — PreseMaker performs the cognitive-depth check by comparing a standard's Bloom's level against the learning outcomes aligned to that standard, not against the materials or assessments.
  • The Role of Learning Outcomes in the Check — Learning outcomes serve as the sole reference point for determining whether a standard's cognitive depth is being met.
  • How the Comparison Determines a Gap — The check applies a straightforward rule: if the standard's Bloom's level exceeds the highest Bloom's level of its aligned learning outcomes, a gap is flagged.
  • What the Cognitive-Depth Check Does Not Evaluate — The cognitive-depth check is deliberately scoped to learning outcomes only and does not assess the Bloom's levels of assignments, quiz items, or activities.

Guides learners in interpreting cognitive-depth flags in the coverage view and understanding how to strengthen course design where learning outcomes fall short of the depth a standard demands.

  • Strategies for Strengthening Learning Outcomes to Close Depth Gaps — Closing a cognitive-depth gap requires rewriting existing learning outcomes or adding new ones that reflect the Bloom's level the standard demands.
  • Avoiding Common Misreads When Addressing Depth Gaps — Learners sometimes confuse the cognitive-depth gap with a coverage gap and attempt to fix it by adding assessments or activities rather than revising outcomes.
