Module: Building a Standards Library The Standards Library is a reusable collection of academic standards that you assemble with AI and then map your course to. This capability is newer and is currently labeled "System in Development," so describe it as an evolving feature whose requirements are still being finalized. What the Standards Library is. It is a library of standards you build once and can reuse across courses. You assemble it with AI rather than typing every standard by hand. Building the library with AI. You give PreseMaker either a description of a standards framework or a real standards source — a web page address, or text pasted from a standards site (for example a state standards site such as CPALMS). The AI reads what you provide and extracts the standards into your library, where you review and save them. The Verified marker means provenance, not confidence. Each standard is marked Verified or Unverified based on WHERE it came from — not on how confident the AI is. Standards extracted from a real source you provided (a URL or pasted text) are marked Verified. Standards the AI produced from only the framework's name, with no source to read, are Unverified — their codes and wording may be approximate or even invented, so you should check them against the official source before relying on them. The marker is not a confidence score, a percentage, or a "pending approval" state. Cognitive level on each standard. A standard can carry a Bloom's Taxonomy cognitive level. This is used later by the Standards Coverage view to check whether your course teaches the standard at the depth it demands. Mapping your course to standards. Once the library exists, you map your course's topics and learning outcomes to the relevant standards, which is what makes coverage reporting possible.
