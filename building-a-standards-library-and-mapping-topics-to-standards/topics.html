Building a Standards Library and Mapping Topics to Standards — Module Topics

What Is the Standards Library

Introduces the Standards Library as a reusable collection of academic standards built once and applied across multiple courses. Explains its purpose and its current 'System in Development' status as an evolving feature.
  • Definition of the Standards Library — The Standards Library is a reusable collection of academic standards that you build once and apply across multiple courses.
  • Purpose of the Standards Library — The Standards Library exists to connect your course content to recognized academic standards, enabling systematic coverage tracking.
  • AI-Assisted Assembly — Rather than typing every standard by hand, you provide PreseMaker with a standards source and the AI extracts the standards into your library for you.
  • 'System in Development' Status — The Standards Library is a newer capability currently labeled 'System in Development,' meaning it is an evolving feature whose requirements are still being finalized.
  • Reusability Across Courses — One of the Standards Library's core advantages is that a single library can be mapped to multiple courses without being rebuilt each time.

Building the Library with AI

Covers how learners provide PreseMaker with a standards framework description, a web page URL, or pasted text from a standards source so the AI can extract and populate the library. Describes the review and save workflow after extraction.
  • Providing Input to the AI — To build a Standards Library, you give PreseMaker one of several types of input that the AI uses to identify and extract standards.
  • How the AI Extracts Standards — Once you provide input, PreseMaker's AI reads the material and extracts individual standards into your library automatically, rather than requiring you to enter each standard by hand.
  • Reviewing Extracted Standards — After extraction, you review the standards the AI has populated before saving them to your library.
  • Saving Standards to the Library — After reviewing the extracted standards, you save them to complete the library-building workflow, making the standards available for reuse across multiple courses.
  • The Verified vs. Unverified Marker — Each standard in the library is labeled either Verified or Unverified based on the source the AI used to produce it, not on the AI's confidence level.

Understanding the Verified and Unverified Markers

Explains that the Verified marker indicates the provenance of a standard — whether it came from a real source provided by the user or was generated by the AI from a framework name alone. Clarifies that Unverified standards may contain approximate or invented codes and wording that must be checked against official sources.
  • What the Verified/Unverified Marker Actually Means — The Verified and Unverified markers indicate the provenance — the origin — of a standard, not a confidence score or approval status.
  • When a Standard Is Marked Verified — A standard receives the Verified marker when it was extracted from a real source that you provided to the AI, such as a URL or pasted text from an official standards site.
  • When a Standard Is Marked Unverified — A standard is marked Unverified when the AI generated it from a framework name alone, with no actual source document or web page provided for it to read.
  • Why Unverified Standards Can Contain Invented Content — When an AI generates standards from a framework name without a source to read, it draws on its training data, which may be incomplete, outdated, or simply inaccurate for specific standard codes and language.
  • How to Respond to Unverified Standards — Because Unverified standards may be approximate or invented, the appropriate response is to verify each one against the official published source before using it in your course mapping.
  • What the Marker Does Not Tell You — It is important to understand the boundaries of what the Verified and Unverified markers communicate so you do not over- or under-rely on them.

Cognitive Levels on Standards

Describes how each standard in the library can carry a Bloom's Taxonomy cognitive level. Explains how this level is later used by the Standards Coverage view to assess whether course content addresses a standard at the required depth.
  • What Bloom's Taxonomy Cognitive Levels Represent — Bloom's Taxonomy organizes cognitive learning into a hierarchy of levels, from basic recall up to complex evaluation and creation.
  • Assigning a Cognitive Level to a Standard in the Library — Each standard stored in the Standards Library can carry a designated Bloom's Taxonomy cognitive level as one of its properties.
  • How Cognitive Level Drives Depth Requirements — The cognitive level attached to a standard sets a benchmark for how deeply course content must address that standard.
  • Role of Cognitive Level in the Standards Coverage View — The Standards Coverage view uses each standard's cognitive level to assess whether mapped course content meets the standard at the required depth.
  • Practical Implications of Getting the Cognitive Level Right — Accurately assigning cognitive levels at the library stage is critical because errors will propagate into every course that maps to those standards.

Mapping Topics and Learning Outcomes to Standards

Walks through the process of linking a course's topics and learning outcomes to the relevant standards in the library. Explains how this mapping enables accurate standards coverage reporting across a course.
  • What Mapping Means in the Standards Library — Mapping is the process of linking your course's topics and learning outcomes to the relevant standards stored in your Standards Library.
  • Why Mapping Enables Standards Coverage Reporting — The mapping between course content and standards is the foundation that makes accurate standards coverage reporting possible.
  • Mapping Topics to Standards — Individual course topics can be linked directly to one or more standards in the library to indicate which standards that topic addresses.
  • Mapping Learning Outcomes to Standards — Learning outcomes can also be mapped to standards, providing a more precise alignment between what learners are expected to demonstrate and the standard being addressed.
  • Using Cognitive Level in the Mapping Process — Standards in the library can carry a Bloom's Taxonomy cognitive level, and this level plays a role in how the coverage view evaluates your mapped content.
  • Reviewing and Confirming Your Mappings — After mapping topics and outcomes to standards, reviewing the full set of connections ensures completeness and accuracy before relying on coverage reports.
