How the Cognitive-Depth Check Works

▶

When you design a course in PreseMaker, the platform does more than check whether your standards are covered by some piece of content — it also checks whether the thinking your learning outcomes demand is deep enough to match what each standard actually requires. This is what the cognitive-depth check does. Understanding how it works in detail is essential, because a misreading of the check leads instructional designers to take the wrong corrective action — often adding more materials when the real fix is rewriting a learning outcome.

The cognitive-depth check is grounded in Bloom's Taxonomy, a widely used framework that classifies educational goals into six hierarchical levels of cognitive complexity. From least to most demanding, those levels are: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, and Create. Each standard in PreseMaker carries a designated Bloom's level that reflects the depth of thinking the standard is meant to develop. A standard written at the Analyze level, for example, is signaling that learners must do more than recall or explain — they must break material apart, examine relationships, and draw inferences.

What a Cognitive-Depth Gap Is

A cognitive-depth gap occurs when no learning outcome aligned to a standard reaches the Bloom's level that standard requires. PreseMaker flags this condition visually in the coverage view, marking the affected standard so the course designer knows a mismatch exists. The flag is a diagnostic signal, not a content warning. It is telling you that your learning outcomes need attention — specifically, that the highest level of thinking any of your outcomes asks learners to perform falls short of what the standard demands.

It is worth being precise about what the flag is not telling you. It is not telling you that you need another assignment, another quiz, or another activity. Those materials may be excellent, but they play no role whatsoever in triggering or clearing this particular flag. The cognitive-depth check is exclusively a comparison between the standard's designated Bloom's level and the Bloom's level of the learning outcomes aligned to it.

Consider a concrete example. Suppose a standard is tagged at the Evaluate level — learners should be able to judge the quality or validity of something using established criteria. If the only learning outcomes aligned to that standard are written at the Understand level (for instance, "Learners will explain the characteristics of X"), PreseMaker will flag a cognitive-depth gap. The gap exists because the most cognitively demanding thing any aligned outcome asks of the learner is to explain, while the standard requires them to evaluate. No number of additional readings, videos, or discussion boards will close that gap, because none of those materials are part of the comparison.

The Comparison Is Between Standard and Learning Outcomes — Not Materials

PreseMaker's cognitive-depth check operates on a specific, narrow comparison: the Bloom's level assigned to a standard on one side, and the highest Bloom's level found among all learning outcomes that are aligned to that standard on the other. Assignments, quiz items, rubrics, readings, and activities are entirely outside this calculation.

This distinction matters enormously in practice. A common mistake is to see a cognitive-depth gap flag and respond by adding a higher-order quiz question or a more challenging assignment. The designer may reason: "If I add an analysis-level quiz item, the system should see that learners are being asked to analyze." But PreseMaker does not look at quiz items during the cognitive-depth check. The flag will remain unchanged because the quiz item is not a learning outcome, and the check only reads learning outcomes.

The logic behind this design is sound. Learning outcomes are the explicit, measurable statements of what learners are expected to know and be able to do by the end of a module or course. They are the authoritative record of instructional intent. Materials and assessments are the means of achieving and evidencing those outcomes, but they are not the outcomes themselves. By anchoring the cognitive-depth check to outcomes rather than materials, PreseMaker keeps the instructional design conversation at the right level of abstraction.

The Role of Learning Outcomes in the Check

When PreseMaker evaluates a standard for cognitive depth, it looks at every learning outcome that has been aligned to that standard and identifies the highest Bloom's level present among them. This single highest-level outcome is what gets compared against the standard's required level. The presence of lower-level outcomes does not penalize the standard — only the peak matters.

This has an important practical implication: a single, well-written high-level outcome can satisfy the cognitive-depth requirement for a standard, even if several other aligned outcomes operate at lower Bloom's levels. For example, suppose a standard requires Analysis. You might have three learning outcomes aligned to it:

  • Outcome A: "Learners will recall the key principles of X." (Remember)
  • Outcome B: "Learners will explain how X applies in context Y." (Understand)
  • Outcome C: "Learners will break down a case study to identify where principles of X are being applied correctly and where they are violated." (Analyze)

PreseMaker sees that the highest Bloom's level among the three outcomes is Analyze, which matches the standard's required level. No cognitive-depth gap is flagged. Outcomes A and B are lower, but they do not create a problem — they may represent scaffolding steps toward the higher-order objective.

The flip side is equally important, and it is easy to get backwards: if no learning outcomes are aligned to a standard at all, PreseMaker does not flag a cognitive-depth gap. The check needs two known levels to compare — the standard's and the deepest aligned outcome's — and with no aligned outcomes the second value is simply unknown. Rather than report a gap it cannot substantiate, the view reports no gap and the depth of that standard remains undetermined. This is worth remembering, because an absence of the warning marker does not by itself mean a standard is covered at the right depth; it may mean nothing has been aligned to it yet. Aligning at least one appropriately leveled learning outcome is what makes the depth check meaningful in the first place.

How the Comparison Determines a Gap

The decision logic PreseMaker uses is a straightforward binary comparison. It can be summarized cleanly:

Standard's Bloom's Level Highest Aligned Outcome's Bloom's Level Result
Evaluate Evaluate No gap — levels are equal
Evaluate Create No gap — outcome exceeds standard
Evaluate Analyze Gap flagged — outcome falls short
Analyze Apply Gap flagged — outcome falls short
Apply Apply No gap — levels are equal
Any level No outcomes aligned No gap flagged — depth is undetermined, nothing to compare

When the highest aligned outcome level is equal to or greater than the standard's level, no gap is flagged. When the standard's level is higher than the highest aligned outcome level, the gap is flagged. There is no sliding scale, no partial credit, and no percentage score indicating how close you are. Either the cognitive-depth requirement is met or it is not. A standard requiring Evaluate that is only covered by Analyze-level outcomes has a gap, even though Analyze is one level away — just as a standard requiring Evaluate covered only by Remember-level outcomes has a gap, despite the much larger distance. In both cases, the coverage view shows the same gap flag.

This binary design keeps the system unambiguous. Designers always know exactly what they are looking at: a gap that needs to be resolved, or no gap. There is no room for interpretation about whether 80% depth is "good enough."

What the Cognitive-Depth Check Does Not Evaluate

Several things are explicitly outside the scope of the cognitive-depth check, and being clear about them prevents wasted effort during course revision.

  • Assignments, quiz items, and activities are irrelevant to this check. Adding, removing, or modifying any of these has zero effect on whether a cognitive-depth gap is flagged. This is the single most important misconception to avoid. Designers who try to resolve a gap by creating a higher-order assessment question will find the flag unchanged the next time they view the coverage report.
  • The check produces no numeric score or percentage. PreseMaker does not report that a standard is "75% of the way" to meeting its cognitive-depth requirement. The result is binary: gap or no gap. This means you cannot prioritize gaps by severity using the depth check alone — every flagged standard has a gap that must be resolved at the outcome level.
  • The check does not evaluate the quality of materials aligned to a standard. A standard could have dozens of carefully designed, high-quality activities and still carry a cognitive-depth gap if the learning outcomes themselves are written at too low a Bloom's level. Conversely, a standard with very few materials but one well-written high-level outcome will show no cognitive-depth gap.

Resolving a cognitive-depth gap always means taking action at the learning-outcome level. In practice, this means one of two things: either rewriting an existing learning outcome so that it uses higher-order action verbs and demands deeper cognitive work, or adding a new learning outcome that is explicitly aligned to the standard at the required Bloom's level. For instance, if a standard requires Evaluate and your current outcomes top out at Understand, you might rewrite your best outcome using verbs like judge, critique, justify, or defend — all associated with the Evaluate level — so that the comparison PreseMaker performs now finds an outcome that meets or exceeds the standard's requirement.

Understanding the cognitive-depth check at this level of detail allows instructional designers to act efficiently. When the flag appears, the question to ask is not "What content am I missing?" but rather "Are any of my learning outcomes for this standard written at the level the standard requires?" That reframing points directly to the correct corrective action and keeps course revision focused, purposeful, and aligned with the underlying logic of the system.
