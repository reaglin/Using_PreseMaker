Module Topics

Introduction to the Standards Coverage View

Overview of the Standards Coverage view in PreseMaker, including its current development status and the headline summary it provides about a course's standards alignment.
  • What the Standards Coverage View Is — The Standards Coverage view is a feature in PreseMaker that shows how well your course materials cover the standards you have mapped, including whether coverage reaches the required cognitive depth.
  • The Headline Summary — At the top of the Standards Coverage view, a headline summarizes the overall alignment picture for the entire course at a glance.
  • How Standards Are Displayed as Rows — Each mapped standard appears as its own row in the view, presenting several pieces of information together in a structured layout.
  • The Tiered Status System — Rather than a numeric score or percentage bar, each standard receives one of a small set of tiered status labels that describe the quality of its coverage.
  • Cognitive-Depth Checking Within the View — The Standards Coverage view automatically flags any standard whose coverage is shallower than the cognitive level the standard itself demands, creating a visible depth-gap warning.

Understanding What the Coverage View Displays

Explanation of the information shown for each mapped standard, including its code, statement, cognitive level, aligned item counts, and overall status.
  • The Standard Row Layout — Each mapped standard appears as its own row in the Coverage view, consolidating all key information in one place.
  • The Cognitive (Bloom's) Level Column — Each standard row includes the cognitive level associated with that standard, drawn from Bloom's Taxonomy.
  • The Tiered Status Field — Each standard is assigned one of a small set of status tiers rather than a numeric score, clearly categorizing how well it is currently covered.
  • The Coverage Headline Summary — A headline at the top of the Coverage view summarizes the overall state of standards coverage across the entire course.
  • The Inline Cognitive-Depth Gap Flag — When a standard's coverage does not reach the cognitive depth its level requires, the row displays an inline warning marker.

The Tiered Status System

Breakdown of the four status tiers — not aligned, not addressed, practiced only, and assessed — and the key distinction between practiced-only and assessed coverage.
  • Overview of the Four Status Tiers — Every mapped standard is assigned one of four discrete status tiers rather than a numeric score or percentage.
  • Not Aligned — A standard is marked 'not aligned' when no course materials have been mapped to it at all.
  • Not Addressed — A standard is marked 'not addressed' when it has been mapped to the course but no materials yet practice or assess it.
  • Practiced Only — A standard reaches 'practiced only' status when it is covered through an activity or assignment but has not yet been assessed by a quiz item.
  • Assessed — A standard achieves 'assessed' status when at least one quiz or assessment item directly covers it.
  • The Key Distinction: Practiced Only vs. Assessed — The most instructionally significant boundary in the tiered system is the line between 'practiced only' and 'assessed.'

The Cognitive-Depth Check

How the coverage view flags standards whose alignment is shallower than the standard's required Bloom's level, including where the flag appears and how the required depth is determined.
  • What a Cognitive-Depth Gap Is — A cognitive-depth gap occurs when a standard's coverage is shallower than the cognitive level the standard itself demands.
  • How the Required Depth Is Determined — The required cognitive depth is drawn directly from the standard's own assigned Bloom's level, not from any external program setting.
  • Where the Depth-Gap Flag Appears — When a depth gap is detected, the flag surfaces in two places within the coverage view: inline on the standard's row and in the overall headline summary.
  • What the Depth Check Is Not — The cognitive-depth check is a built-in, automatic part of the coverage view rather than a separately triggered or exportable feature.
  • Relationship Between the Depth Flag and Tiered Status — A cognitive-depth gap flag is distinct from a standard's tiered status and can appear alongside any status tier that involves some coverage.

Strengthening Shallow or Incomplete Coverage

Strategies for addressing depth gaps and unassessed standards by adding or upgrading course materials to meet the required cognitive level of each standard.
  • Recognizing When Coverage Needs Strengthening — Coverage gaps appear when a standard's status is 'not addressed,' 'practiced only,' or flagged with a depth gap warning.
  • Understanding the Required Cognitive Level — The target cognitive depth for each standard comes directly from the standard's own assigned Bloom's level, not from any program-wide setting.
  • Moving a Standard from 'Practiced Only' to 'Assessed' — The most direct way to close a 'practiced only' gap is to add at least one quiz item that is aligned to the standard.
  • Upgrading Materials to the Correct Bloom's Level — When a depth gap is flagged, existing materials must be supplemented or replaced with content that targets the standard at its required Bloom's level.
  • Addressing a 'Not Addressed' Standard — A standard with 'not addressed' status requires the creation of new course materials from scratch, since nothing currently practices or assesses it.
  • Verifying That Gaps Are Resolved — After adding or upgrading materials, the coverage view updates each standard's status and depth-gap flag to reflect the new alignment.
