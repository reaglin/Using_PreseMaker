Understanding the Course-Module-Content Structure — Module Topics

The PreseMaker Hierarchy Overview

Introduces the overall organizational structure of PreseMaker, explaining how courses, modules, topics, and generated materials fit together as a unified system.
  • What Is PreseMaker? — PreseMaker is an e-learning authoring tool built around a clear, layered organizational system for creating and managing educational content.
  • The Four Levels of the Hierarchy — PreseMaker organizes content into nested levels: Courses contain Modules, Modules contain Topics, and Topics contain Subtopics. The materials PreseMaker produces are outputs generated from these levels.
  • How Courses Serve as the Top-Level Container — A Course is the broadest organizational unit in PreseMaker, providing the overarching framework under which all other content is grouped.
  • Modules as Thematic Groupings — Modules break a Course into manageable sections, each focused on a distinct theme, unit, or instructional goal.
  • Topics as the Core Instructional Unit — Topics are where individual lessons are defined; each Topic represents a single, focused concept or learning objective within a Module.
  • Generated Materials as the Output Layer — Generated Materials are the tangible content assets that PreseMaker produces from a Topic, such as presentation slides, speaker notes, or assessments.
  • The Hierarchy as a Unified System — The four levels of the PreseMaker hierarchy are not isolated layers — they work together as a unified system where each level informs and supports the others.

Understanding Courses and Modules

Explores the relationship between courses and modules, clarifying how modules serve as the primary building blocks organized within a course.
  • What Is a Course? — A course is the top-level container in PreseMaker, representing the broadest organizational unit for a subject or learning program.
  • What Is a Module? — A module is the primary building block within a course, representing a focused section or unit of instruction.
  • The Parent-Child Relationship Between Courses and Modules — Courses and modules share a hierarchical parent-child relationship, where the course acts as the parent and modules act as its children.
  • Why Modules Are the Primary Building Blocks — Modules are considered the primary building blocks of a course because they are the main units through which instructional content is structured and delivered.
  • Organizing Modules Within a Course — Modules within a course can be arranged in a deliberate sequence to guide learners through content in a logical and progressive order.

Topics and Generated Materials

Examines how topics nest within modules and how PreseMaker generates content materials from those topics, connecting structure to output.
  • Topics as Building Blocks Within Modules — Topics are the smallest structural unit within a module, each representing a focused concept or skill area.
  • How Topics Nest Inside Modules — PreseMaker organizes topics in a hierarchical relationship, where every topic belongs to exactly one parent module.
  • From Topic to Generated Content — PreseMaker uses the information defined in a topic — its title, summary, and source content — to automatically generate learning materials.
  • Types of Materials Generated from Topics — For each topic, PreseMaker can produce a variety of content elements that together form the learner-facing material.
  • The Structural Chain: Course → Module → Topic → Materials — Understanding the full hierarchy helps authors see how a top-level course ultimately produces granular learning content through a series of nested relationships.
  • Connecting Structure to Output — The relationship between topics and generated materials is what bridges course design with the actual learner experience in PreseMaker.

How PreseMaker Stores Files on Your Computer

Describes where and how PreseMaker saves files locally on the author's computer, ensuring users understand file locations and ownership of their work.
  • Local Storage: Where PreseMaker Saves Your Files — PreseMaker saves all generated course materials directly to your local computer rather than storing them exclusively in the cloud.
  • Default File Location and Folder Structure — PreseMaker organizes saved files into a predictable folder hierarchy that mirrors the course-module-content structure used inside the application.
  • File Formats Used by PreseMaker — Generated materials are saved in common, widely supported file formats so they can be opened, edited, and shared without requiring special software.
  • Ownership and Access Rights to Your Files — All files saved by PreseMaker belong entirely to the user; the application does not retain ownership or impose usage restrictions on generated content.
  • Locating and Verifying Your Saved Files — PreseMaker provides straightforward ways to confirm where a file has been saved and to open its containing folder directly from within the application.
  • Managing and Backing Up Your PreseMaker Files — Because files live on your local computer, it is the user's responsibility to back up and manage those files to prevent accidental loss.

Accessing and Owning Your Work

Reinforces that authors retain full ownership of all generated content and explains how to reliably locate and manage their files at any time.
  • You Own Everything You Create — All content generated through PreseMaker belongs entirely to you, the author, with no restrictions or platform lock-in.
  • Where PreseMaker Stores Your Files — PreseMaker saves all generated content to a designated location on your local computer, making files easy to find and manage.
  • Locating Your Files at Any Time — PreseMaker provides consistent, reliable methods for finding your saved content whenever you need it.
  • Managing and Organizing Your Generated Content — Keeping your files organized ensures long-term accessibility and makes it easier to revisit or build upon previous work.
  • Accessing Your Work Across Sessions — Your generated content persists between sessions, so progress and completed materials are never lost when you close the application.
